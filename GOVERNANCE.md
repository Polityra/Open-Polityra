# Project Governance

## 1. Purpose

This document describes the interim governance of OpenPolityra an independent open-source research and technology initiative. Its purpose is to make responsibility, decision-making and accountability understandable while the project is small and volunteer-led.

This document does not create a company, association, foundation, employment relationship or legal partnership. The project is not currently presented as a registered Finnish foundation or as an organisation representing the Finnish Government.

## 2. Mission and boundaries

The project develops and studies open tools for analysing public information and exploring scenarios relevant to Finland. It supports informed human judgement and research. It does not exercise public authority, make political decisions, recommend votes, conduct party campaigning or act on behalf of a public institution.

The project follows the principles in [README.md](README.md), [DATA_POLICY.md](DATA_POLICY.md), [MODEL_POLICY.md](MODEL_POLICY.md) and [CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md](CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md).

## 3. Interim roles

Roles are responsibilities, not honorary titles or guarantees of authority.

### Project initiator

The initiator coordinates the early project, maintains the public roadmap, convenes discussions and helps recruit contributors. The initiator should not unilaterally approve changes that create legal, financial or security commitments for an entity that does not exist.

### Maintainers

Maintainers are trusted contributors with repository permissions. They are responsible for code review, issue triage, releases, access control and enforcement of repository policies. Maintainer status should be based on demonstrated contribution, judgement, availability and willingness to take responsibility.

### Workstream leads

Workstream leads coordinate a defined area, such as software, data, AI evaluation, economic research, security or documentation. They propose priorities and review relevant contributions, but do not automatically gain authority over other workstreams.

### Contributors and reviewers

Contributors submit work and take part in discussion. Reviewers provide technical or research review. Participation does not automatically confer maintainer status, governance rights or ownership of the project.

## 4. Decision-making

The project should prefer open discussion and documented consensus. When consensus cannot be reached, maintainers may make a decision within their delegated operational responsibilities and record the reasoning.

Decisions should:

1. identify the problem and options considered;
2. describe evidence, assumptions and trade-offs;
3. identify affected workstreams and risks;
4. record dissenting views when materially relevant;
5. state who approved the decision and when;
6. be revisited if important assumptions change.

Significant architectural choices should be recorded as Architecture Decision Records (ADRs) under `docs/decisions/`. Changes to project mission, licence, governance, political-neutrality commitments, data policy or model policy require a public proposal and a documented maintainer decision. Where possible, allow a reasonable comment period before adoption.

## 5. Reviews and approvals

- Routine code changes require at least one maintainer approval and passing required checks.
- Changes affecting security or personal data require review by a maintainer with relevant competence.
- Changes to evaluation methodology require review by someone familiar with the relevant research domain where possible.
- Changes to project-wide policy require public discussion and a recorded decision.
- A person should not be the sole reviewer of a change where they have a material conflict of interest.

The project may adopt more formal approval rules as the contributor community grows.

## 6. Conflicts of interest

Contributors and maintainers should disclose interests that could reasonably affect how their work is perceived. Examples include paid work for an entity discussed in a report, funding from an interested organisation, a direct financial interest in a recommendation, or a formal political or commercial role relevant to the work.

A disclosed conflict does not automatically disqualify a person. The project should decide whether disclosure, an independent review, recusal or another safeguard is appropriate and record the decision where material.

## 7. Funding and financial transparency

Until a legal entity and approved financial controls exist, the project should not imply that it can receive tax-deductible donations, issue official receipts, employ staff or enter into binding contracts on behalf of a foundation.

Any funds accepted for project activity must have a clearly identified recipient, purpose, accounting record and authorised person. The project should not collect money into a personal account as though it were a foundation or registered organisation. Sponsorships and grants must not buy control over research outcomes or suppress material findings.

If a foundation or other non-profit entity is established, it should publish appropriate financial reports, funding sources, grant conditions and conflict-of-interest information, subject to applicable law and privacy requirements.

## 8. Future non-profit foundation

A future independent non-profit foundation is a possibility, not a current legal fact or commitment. Before establishment, the project should obtain qualified Finnish legal and accounting advice on legal form, purpose, founding assets, registration, board duties, supervision, taxation, fundraising, employment and intellectual property.

If a foundation is established, its governing documents must define its legal purpose and powers. The foundation's board and the open-source project's maintainers should have clearly separated responsibilities. The foundation should protect the project's independence and public-interest purpose, not direct research conclusions to satisfy a funder or political stakeholder.

No draft document in this repository is a substitute for approved statutes, legal advice or formal registration.

## 9. External partnerships

Any collaboration with a university, company, public authority, non-profit or other external party must have a clear scope and public description where disclosure is permitted. The project must not imply endorsement or official affiliation without written authorisation.

Partnerships must not override research independence, source transparency, human oversight or the project's safety boundaries.

## 10. Amendments and review

This governance model is interim and should be reviewed when the project has a stable contributor base, receives funding, enters formal partnerships or establishes a legal entity. Proposed amendments should be discussed publicly and recorded in the repository.
